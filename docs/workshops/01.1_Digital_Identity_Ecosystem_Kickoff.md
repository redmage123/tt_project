# Workshop 1.1: Digital Identity Ecosystem Kickoff
## Phase 1: Digital Foundations

**Workshop Code:** WS 1.1
**Primary Component:** Electronic Identity (e-ID) Program
**Duration:** 2 days
**Timing:** Month 1, Week 3-4
**Phase Theme:** "Building Trust & Infrastructure"

---

## Executive Summary

This inaugural workshop launches the e-ID program by bringing together all key stakeholders to establish a shared vision, define technical requirements, and align on use cases for Trinidad & Tobago's electronic identity system. The workshop sets the foundation for creating a secure, inclusive, and interoperable e-ID that can serve as the authentication backbone for digital government services and position Trinidad & Tobago as a regional leader.

**Key Outcome:** Stakeholder consensus on e-ID architecture, use cases, and implementation roadmap with embedded gender and inclusion principles.

---

## Workshop Objectives

### Primary Objectives
1. **Establish Shared Vision:** Align all stakeholders on the strategic importance and potential of e-ID for Trinidad & Tobago
2. **Define Use Cases:** Identify priority government and private sector use cases for e-ID deployment
3. **Technical Foundation:** Agree on core technical architecture, standards, and integration requirements
4. **Regional Positioning:** Explore cross-border applications and position T&T as Caribbean e-ID leader
5. **Inclusion by Design:** Embed gender, accessibility, and inclusion principles from the outset

### Secondary Objectives
- Build relationships among the four ID provider organizations
- Introduce EU best practices from Estonia and other digital leaders
- Establish governance structure for e-ID program
- Identify pilot opportunities for early implementation

## Detailed Agenda

### DAY 1: Vision, Context & Use Cases

#### Morning Session (9:00 AM - 12:30 PM)

**9:00 - 9:30 | Opening & Context Setting**
- Welcome by MPAAI Minister/Permanent Secretary
- Project overview by CAF
- EU partnership remarks
- Workshop objectives and agenda overview

**9:30 - 10:30 | The Global e-ID Landscape**
- Presentation: "Digital Identity as Economic Infrastructure" (30 min)
  - Global trends in e-ID adoption
  - Economic impact data
  - Regional comparisons
- **Case Study: Estonia's e-ID Success Story** (20 min)
  - Presented by Estonian expert
  - 20 years of e-governance
  - Lessons learned and pitfalls avoided
- Q&A (10 min)

**10:30 - 10:45 | BREAK**

**10:45 - 12:30 | Trinidad & Tobago e-ID Vision**
- **Panel Discussion:** "Building Digital Trust in Trinidad & Tobago" (45 min)
  - Representatives from all four ID providers
  - Current state of identity systems
  - Challenges with siloed identity
  - Opportunities for integration

- **Interactive Exercise:** "Identity Journey Mapping" (45 min)
  - Small groups map citizen interactions with government that require ID
  - Identify pain points, duplication, security gaps
  - Report back to plenary (15 min)

- **Synthesis:** Emerging themes and priorities (15 min)

**12:30 - 1:30 PM | LUNCH**

#### Afternoon Session (1:30 PM - 5:00 PM)

**1:30 - 3:00 | Use Case Development**
- **Presentation:** "Defining e-ID Use Cases" (15 min)
  - Framework for use case analysis
  - Examples from EU member states

- **Breakout Groups:** Use Case Workshops (1 hour)
  - **Group 1:** Government-to-Citizen (G2C) Services
    - Healthcare access, education enrollment, social benefits
  - **Group 2:** Government-to-Business (G2B) Services
    - Business registration, tax filing, procurement
  - **Group 3:** Cross-Border & Regional Services
    - Travel, education, professional credentials
  - **Group 4:** Sensitive/High-Security Services
    - Law enforcement, courts, sensitive personal data access

  Each group identifies:
  - Top 3 priority use cases
  - Security requirements
  - User experience considerations
  - Gender/inclusion implications

- **Group Reports & Discussion** (30 min)

**3:00 - 3:15 | BREAK**

**3:15 - 4:30 | Gender, Inclusion & Digital Rights**
- **Presentation:** "Inclusive e-ID Design" (20 min)
  - Universal design principles
  - Gender considerations (e.g., safe access for GBV survivors)
  - Accessibility for persons with disabilities
  - Digital literacy barriers
  - Privacy and data protection

- **Case Study:** EU Digital Rights Approaches (15 min)
  - GDPR implications
  - Right to be forgotten
  - Data minimization

- **Interactive Discussion:** "Who Gets Left Behind?" (30 min)
  - Identifying vulnerable populations
  - Designing for universal access
  - Safeguards against discrimination

**4:30 - 5:00 | Day 1 Wrap-up**
- Synthesis of key insights
- Preview of Day 2
- Reflection exercise

## Key Discussion Questions

### Strategic Questions
- What does success look like for Trinidad & Tobago's e-ID program in 3 years? 5 years?
- How can e-ID position T&T as a regional digital leader?
- What is the role of private sector in the e-ID ecosystem?

### Technical Questions
- Should T&T adopt a centralized or federated identity model?
- What authentication levels are needed for different use cases?
- How do we ensure interoperability with Caribbean neighbors?
- What role should mobile devices play in e-ID access?

### Governance Questions
- How do we balance security with user convenience?
- What safeguards prevent misuse or surveillance?
- How do we ensure inclusive access while maintaining security?
- What is the relationship between the four ID providers in the new system?

### Implementation Questions
- What use cases should be prioritized for pilot?
- What is a realistic timeline for deployment?
- What capacity building is needed within government?
- How do we build public trust and adoption?

## Risk Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Caribbean partners unable to attend | Invite 4-5 countries, expect 2-3; offer virtual participation option |
| EU expert cancellation | Have backup experts identified; pre-record video presentation if needed |
| Lack of consensus on architecture | Frame as "options analysis" rather than final decision; allow more time for discussion |
| Low engagement from ID providers | Pre-workshop individual meetings to build buy-in; emphasize benefits to each agency |
| Technical jargon alienates policy participants | Balance technical and strategic content; use clear language and analogies |
