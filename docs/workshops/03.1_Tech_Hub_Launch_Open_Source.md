# Workshop 3.1: Tech Hub Launch - Open Source & Standards
## Phase 3: Digital Innovation

**Workshop Code:** WS 3.1
**Primary Component:** Data Centre Expansion (Tech Hub #1 of 5)
**Duration:** 2 days
**Timing:** Month 7, Week 2-3
**Phase Theme:** "Empowering the Digital Ecosystem"

---

## Executive Summary

This workshop marks a critical transition—transforming Trinidad & Tobago's data centre from traditional government infrastructure into an innovation hub that empowers the broader digital ecosystem. Building on the infrastructure planning from Workshop 1.2, this workshop activates the "tech hub" vision by establishing sandbox environments, open-source governance, and partnerships with universities, developers, and the Caribbean community. This is the first of five tech hub workshops and sets the foundation for emerging technology exploration (AI/ML/blockchain/IoT in WS 3.2) and private sector engagement (WS 3.3).

**Key Outcome:** Operational tech hub framework with sandbox environments, open-source contribution model, and active developer/academic community engagement.

---

## Workshop Objectives

### Primary Objectives
1. **Launch Tech Hub:** Officially activate the data centre as a technology innovation hub
2. **Establish Sandboxes:** Design and deploy sandbox environments for safe experimentation
3. **Open Source Governance:** Create framework for government open-source contributions and consumption
4. **Build Community:** Engage developers, universities, startups in the tech hub ecosystem
5. **Knowledge Sharing:** Position T&T as Caribbean leader in open government technology

### Secondary Objectives
- Adopt open standards and avoid vendor lock-in
- Create pathways for youth employment and skills development
- Establish Caribbean tech hub network
- Design sustainability model for tech hub operations
- Plan for emerging tech workshops (WS 3.2 series)

## Detailed Agenda

### DAY 1: Open Source, Open Standards, Open Government

#### Morning Session (9:00 AM - 12:30 PM)

**9:00 - 9:45 | Opening & Tech Hub Launch**
- **Official Launch Ceremony** (20 min)
  - Ribbon-cutting or symbolic launch of tech hub
  - Minister/Permanent Secretary remarks on innovation vision
  - Tech hub manifesto/charter reading
  - Video: "Tech Hub Vision 2030"

- **Workshop Introduction** (15 min)
  - Objectives and agenda
  - Participant introductions (rapid-fire: name, affiliation, one tech passion)

- **The Tech Hub Concept** (10 min)
  - What is a tech hub vs. traditional data centre?
  - Who can use it and how?
  - What opportunities does it create?

**9:45 - 11:00 | Open Source in Government: Global Movement**
- **Presentation:** "The Case for Open Source in Government" (30 min)
  - Benefits:
    - Cost savings (no licensing fees)
    - Transparency and trust
    - Security through many eyes
    - Avoid vendor lock-in
    - Customization and control
    - Economic development (local tech capacity)
  - Risks and mitigation:
    - Support and maintenance
    - Security vulnerabilities (responsible disclosure)
    - Skills and capacity
  - Global trends: UK, France, Italy, Nordic countries

- **Case Studies:** "Open Government Success Stories" (35 min)
  - **Barcelona, Spain** (15 min)
    - Presented by EU expert
    - Open-source-first policy
    - Barcelona Supercomputing Centre
    - Citizen participation platforms
    - Local tech ecosystem development

  - **GovStack / Digital Public Goods** (10 min)
    - Building blocks approach
    - Open-source government software catalogue
    - X-Road (used in WS 2.1) as open-source example

  - **Nordic Examples** (10 min)
    - Estonia's e-governance stack (largely open-source)
    - Helsinki's open data and developer APIs
    - Developer community engagement models

- **Q&A** (10 min)

**11:00 - 11:15 | BREAK**

**11:15 - 12:30 | Open Standards & Interoperability**
- **Presentation:** "Open Standards as Foundation" (25 min)
  - What are open standards? (publicly available, vendor-neutral specifications)
  - Why they matter (interoperability, competition, innovation)
  - Examples:
    - Web standards (HTML, CSS, HTTP)
    - Data standards (JSON, XML, RDF)
    - API standards (REST, OpenAPI from WS 2.1)
    - Identity standards (OAuth, SAML from WS 1.1 and 2.3)
  - Standard-setting bodies (W3C, IETF, OASIS, ISO)
  - Relationship to open source (standards ≠ open source, but complementary)

- **Interactive Discussion:** "T&T Open Standards Policy" (40 min)
  - Should T&T mandate open standards in government IT?
  - Which standards to adopt? (Build on WS 2.1 decisions)
  - How to enforce compliance?
  - How to contribute to standard development?
  - Draft policy principles:
    - Open standards preferred in procurement
    - Vendor proposals must explain any proprietary formats
    - Government data published in open formats
    - APIs follow open standards (REST, OpenAPI, OAuth)

**12:30 - 1:30 PM | LUNCH**

#### Afternoon Session (1:30 PM - 5:30 PM)

**1:30 - 3:00 | Open Source Governance Framework**
- **Presentation:** "Governing Open Source in Government" (25 min)
  - Two sides of open source in government:
    1. **Consuming open source** (using existing OSS)
    2. **Contributing/producing open source** (publishing government code)

  - Governance questions:
    - What licenses are acceptable? (MIT, Apache, GPL, etc.)
    - How to assess OSS security and quality?
    - How to contribute back to communities?
    - How to publish government-developed software?
    - What support model for OSS in production?
    - Intellectual property considerations
    - Procurement of OSS support services

- **Panel Discussion:** "Open Source in Practice" (30 min)
  - Local developers who use/contribute to OSS
  - Government IT staff
  - University researchers
  - Topics:
    - Current barriers to OSS adoption in T&T government
    - Skills and capacity needs
    - Community support ecosystems
    - Success stories and lessons learned

- **Breakout Workshop:** "T&T Open Source Policy" (40 min)
  - 4-5 groups address different policy dimensions:

    **Group 1: Consuming Open Source**
    - Assessment criteria for OSS (security, community health, documentation)
    - Approval process
    - Support and maintenance strategy
    - Preferred licenses

    **Group 2: Contributing to Open Source**
    - When should government contribute code?
    - Code review and quality standards
    - Licensing choices for government contributions
    - Developer time allocation (government staff contributing on work time)

    **Group 3: Publishing Government Software**
    - What government software should be open-sourced?
    - What should remain proprietary/closed? (Security considerations)
    - Publication platforms (GitHub, GitLab, etc.)
    - Documentation and community management

    **Group 4: Procurement & Sustainability**
    - How to procure open-source support/services?
    - How to avoid vendor lock-in even with OSS?
    - Funding open-source projects government depends on
    - Building local OSS services industry

  Develop draft policy recommendations

- **Report Back** (5 min per group, 25 min total)

**3:00 - 3:15 | BREAK**

**3:15 - 5:00 | Sandbox Environments: Design & Deployment**
- **Presentation:** "Sandbox Architectures for Innovation" (25 min)
  - What is a sandbox? (isolated, safe environment for experimentation)
  - Use cases:
    - Government developers testing new technologies
    - Universities doing research
    - Startups prototyping products
    - Hackathons and innovation challenges
    - Training and skills development
  - Types of sandboxes:
    - Development/testing environments
    - Data sandboxes (synthetic/anonymized data)
    - API sandboxes (test APIs before production)
    - Technology-specific sandboxes (AI/ML, blockchain, IoT - preview WS 3.2)
  - Infrastructure requirements (compute, storage, network isolation)
  - Security and access control

- **Technical Workshop:** "Designing T&T Tech Hub Sandboxes" (60 min)
  - Mixed groups (government, university, developers)

  **Design sandbox environments:**
  - **General Purpose Dev Sandbox:**
    - Cloud instances (VMs, containers)
    - Development tools and frameworks pre-installed
    - Access model (who can use, how to request)
    - Resource quotas (prevent abuse)

  - **Data Sandbox:**
    - Synthetic datasets for testing (population data, geographic data, etc.)
    - Privacy-preserving techniques
    - API access to government data (read-only, non-sensitive)

  - **API Sandbox:**
    - Test versions of government APIs (from WS 2.1)
    - Mock services for cross-border APIs (from WS 2.3 pilots)
    - Documentation and tutorials

  - **Emerging Tech Sandboxes** (preview for WS 3.2):
    - ML/AI sandbox (GPUs, ML frameworks, datasets)
    - Blockchain sandbox (test networks)
    - IoT sandbox (device simulators)

  For each sandbox:
  - Technical architecture
  - Access policies
  - Usage quotas and fair use
  - Support model
  - Cost estimate

  Create sandbox catalogue and access portal mockup

- **Report Back & Discussion** (15 min)

**5:00 - 5:30 | Day 1 Wrap & Networking**
- Summary of open-source policy and sandbox designs
- Homework: Participants brainstorm potential sandbox projects
- Informal networking

## Risk Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Low developer turnout | Heavy community promotion; engage existing tech groups; make it fun and valuable; offer incentives |
| Open-source policy resisted by procurement/legal | Education on benefits; start with permissive policy; EU expert credibility; phased approach |
| Tech hub infrastructure not ready | Have backup plan for demos; focus on community building if infrastructure delayed; virtual sandboxes as interim |
| Universities unable to commit | Start with 1-2 willing universities; build success stories; expand partnerships later |
| Sustainability concerns prevent investment | Emphasize lean startup approach; demonstrate value through quick wins; external funding for initial phase |
| Lack of diversity in tech community | Proactive outreach to women, youth, underrepresented groups; GID Advisory Committee involvement; inclusive event design |
